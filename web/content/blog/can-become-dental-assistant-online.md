Only part of it. You can learn terminology, anatomy, infection-control principles and radiation safety online, and many people do. But you can't learn to pass a scaler at the right angle or hold suction steady over a video call. Dental assisting is a hands-on job, so the programs worth paying for mix online coursework with in-person practice.

## What a screen can and can't teach

The book-learning parts adapt well to online study: tooth numbering and surfaces, dental terms, why sterilization steps happen in a certain order, how radiation dose works, and what a practice management system looks like. Online lessons also let you rewatch something at 11 p.m. as many times as you need.

The physical skills are a different story. Tray setups, instrument transfer, four-handed dentistry, mixing materials, taking impressions and scans, and positioning an x-ray sensor in a real mouth all take repetition with a person there to correct you. You also need practice working with an actual patient who's nervous, gagging or has a jaw that won't stay put.

## Colorado's x-ray rule makes this concrete

To take x-rays in Colorado, you need qualifying radiography training (or a qualifying DANB exam), and the training routes involve hands-on work. A fully online course that says it covers radiography may teach the theory well and still leave you unqualified to take an x-ray. Ask every program directly whether its radiography training meets Colorado's requirement. Our [requirements page](/requirements/) lays out the routes.

## Hybrid programs

Some Colorado schools already work this way. The [Academy for Dental Assisting Careers](/schools/academy-for-dental-assisting-careers/), with locations in Longmont and Greeley, advertises online coursework combined with hands-on sessions held in working dental offices. Career colleges often blend online and campus classes as well. Each school's format is on our [programs comparison](/programs/dental-assistant/).

## When to walk away

Be skeptical if a program has no hands-on component and no externship, or if it promises you'll be "certified" without saying by whom. DANB exams are separate from any school. Also be wary if it never mentions Colorado's radiography rule, can't tell you whether it's approved by the [Colorado Department of Higher Education](/resources/#cdhe), or pushes you to pay today before a deadline that seems to keep moving.

## Where online learning really helps

Online prep is a good fit for DANB exams like RHS and ICE ([more on DANB](/blog/danb-certification-required-colorado/)). It's also how most working assistants handle continuing education. And if you've enrolled but haven't started yet, spending a few weeks on free terminology resources will make the first month much easier.

So use online study for the knowledge and in-person training for the skills. If you're comparing options, our [guide to choosing a school](/blog/choose-dental-assisting-school-thats-right/) has the rest of the questions to ask.

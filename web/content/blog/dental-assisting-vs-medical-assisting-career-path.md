Dental assisting and medical assisting are both quick ways into healthcare, and people considering one usually consider the other. Pay is similar. The real differences are the setting, the pace and how much of your day is spent chairside versus moving between patients.

## At a glance

| | Dental assistant | Medical assistant |
|---|---|---|
| Median pay (BLS, May 2025) | [$48,070/yr ($23.11/hr)](/resources/#bls) | [$45,690/yr ($21.97/hr)](/resources/#bls-medical-assistants) |
| Projected growth, 2025–35 | 7% | 13% |
| Typical workplace | Dental office (91% per BLS) | Physician offices, clinics, hospitals |
| Colorado training | Short programs: about 8–13 weeks | Typically months to 2 years |
| Colorado credential | None required; x-rays need approved training | None required by the state; employers often want certification |

## The work itself

**Dental assistants** mostly work in one place: the dental chair. You're next to the dentist for procedures, anticipating each step, managing suction and instruments, taking x-rays and keeping infection control tight. The work is precise, repetitive in a good way, and very hands-on. [A typical day](/blog/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-dental-assistant-what-to-expect/).

**Medical assistants** move around more. A typical day mixes clinical work (rooming patients, vitals, injections, drawing blood in some offices, EKGs) with admin: scheduling, charting, insurance forms and phone calls. Variety is the appeal. The trade-off is a wider set of skills to learn and sometimes less depth in any one of them.

## Schedules

Dental offices generally run weekday business hours. Many open early and some add evenings or Saturdays, but overnight shifts are almost unheard of. Four-day weeks are common.

Medical assisting depends on the setting. Physician offices look like dental offices, while urgent care and hospital-affiliated clinics may need evenings, weekends and holidays.

## Training in Colorado

Dental assisting has one of the fastest routes into clinical healthcare in the state. Short private programs in Colorado run about 8 to 13 weeks, with evening and weekend options, and cost roughly $3,250 to $5,995 ([cost guide](/blog/much-dental-assistant-training-programs-cost/)). Career colleges offer longer certificates of around 8 to 9 months that include externships and financial aid. [Compare them here](/programs/dental-assistant/).

The one legal requirement worth knowing: in Colorado you need approved radiography training before you can take dental x-rays. Most good programs build that in. [Colorado's requirements](/requirements/).

Medical assisting programs are usually longer, and employers often prefer candidates certified through a national certifying body.

## Pay and growth

Median pay is close, with dental slightly ahead. Medical assisting has higher projected growth nationally (13% versus 7%) and many more total openings. Dental assisting still grows much faster than average, and dental offices in Colorado hire steadily. For state-specific numbers, see our [Colorado dental assistant salary guide](/blog/dental-assistant-salary-colorado/) and [demand report](/blog/the-growing-demand-for-dental-assistants-in-colorado/).

Where you go next also matters. Dental assistants can move into specialty, expanded-duties or office manager roles, or become [hygienists](/blog/the-difference-between-dental-assistant-and-dental-hygienist-careers/). Medical assistants often move toward nursing, phlebotomy or practice management.

## How to choose

Pick **dental assisting** if you want a fast, affordable start, predictable daytime hours, and hands-on work where you get very good at one set of skills.

Pick **medical assisting** if you want variety, a mix of clinical and front-office work, and more settings to choose from.

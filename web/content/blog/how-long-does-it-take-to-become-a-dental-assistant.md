It depends on which route you take. In Colorado it can be as short as two months or as long as two years, and there's no state license to wait for at the end. Once you're trained, you can start applying.

The main routes break down like this:

| Route | How long | When classes meet |
|---|---|---|
| Private occupational school | About 8–12 weeks | Usually Saturdays or evenings |
| Career college certificate | About 8–9 months | Weekdays, on campus, with an externship |
| Community college associate degree | About 2 years | Full-time college schedule |
| Hired and trained on the job | No set length | Whenever you find the job |

You can see which schools offer which format on our [Colorado programs page](/programs/dental-assistant/).

## The short-program route

This is the path most career changers in Colorado take, because it doesn't require quitting your current job. A realistic version goes something like this.

Spend a week or two choosing a school. Ask whether radiography is included, check the start dates and set up a payment plan. Get your CPR/BLS card during that stretch too. Our [school checklist](/blog/choose-dental-assisting-school-thats-right/) helps with the choosing.

Then comes roughly three months of Saturdays (or two weeknights) covering infection control, instruments, chairside assisting, x-rays and patient care, along with a lot of practice on models. Many programs also include time with real patients.

Plenty of students start applying for jobs in the last few weeks of the course, and some leave with an offer from their externship office. If you start the search at the end instead, give it a few weeks.

From deciding to go for it to working chairside, three to four months is a fair estimate.

## What slows people down

Weekend cohorts fill up, so the class you want may not start for a month or two. Calling early helps.

If your program doesn't include Colorado-approved radiography, you'll need to fit that in before you can take x-rays ([here's the rule](/requirements/)).

Some schools want immunization records or a background check before you start, and those can take a while to track down.

The biggest one is everyday life. Work schedules change, a kid gets sick, a car breaks down. Picking a schedule you can realistically stick to, with backup childcare arranged, matters more than finishing a few weeks sooner.

## After the first job

Getting hired is the start of the timeline. Within a year or two, many assistants add a DANB credential, move into a specialty or take on expanded duties, and that's usually when pay starts to climb. [The highest-paying dental assistant jobs](/blog/highest-paying-dental-assistant-job/) shows where that can lead.

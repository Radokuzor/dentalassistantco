Dental assistants and dental hygienists work side by side, wear similar scrubs, and patients often mix them up. They are very different jobs, though, with different training, legal status, daily work and pay. If you're choosing between them, here's how they compare.

## The short version

| | Dental assistant | Dental hygienist |
|---|---|---|
| Typical training | A few months to a year (short programs or college certificates), or on-the-job | Associate degree in dental hygiene (BLS says programs typically take about 3 years) |
| License in Colorado | None; the Dental Board regulates tasks | Required |
| Main work | Supports the dentist chairside, x-rays, sterilization, patient care | Cleanings, periodontal assessment, preventive care, patient education |
| Median pay (BLS, May 2025) | [$48,070/yr](/resources/#bls) | [$98,100/yr](/resources/#bls-hygienists) |
| Projected growth, 2025–35 | 7% | 8% |

## What each one actually does

**Assistants** keep procedures running. They work alongside the dentist during fillings, crowns, extractions and exams: passing instruments, suctioning, taking x-rays, mixing materials, and handling infection control and room turnover. Their day follows the dentist's schedule. [Here's what that day looks like](/blog/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-dental-assistant-what-to-expect/).

**Hygienists** run their own schedule of patients. They clean teeth, remove tartar above and below the gumline, assess gum health, take x-rays, apply preventive treatments and teach patients about home care. The dentist usually stops in for a quick exam at the end of the visit.

In short: an assistant helps the dentist do the dentist's work, and a hygienist does their own clinical work.

## Training and licensing

This is the biggest difference.

A dental assistant in Colorado can start working after on-the-job training, although most employers prefer someone who has already trained. Colorado [doesn't license assistants](/blog/dental-assistant-colorado-licensed/). What matters legally is the specific task. X-rays, for example, require approved radiography training. Many people finish a short program in 8 to 13 weeks and are working within a few months. [Compare Colorado programs](/programs/dental-assistant/).

A dental hygienist must graduate from an accredited dental hygiene program, pass written and clinical board exams, and hold a state license, which requires continuing education to keep. Hygiene programs usually have prerequisites (biology, chemistry, anatomy) and competitive admission. From deciding to getting licensed often takes three years or more.

Colorado's hygienists also have unusual independence. Colorado lets licensed hygienists practice without a dentist's supervision in some settings. The details are in the rules published by the [Colorado Dental Board](/resources/#dental-board).

## Pay and cost

Hygienists earn roughly twice what assistants do, and that reflects the extra training, the license and the independent patient schedule. Hygiene school also costs far more and takes years longer, usually without much chance to work full time alongside it.

Assisting pays sooner. A Colorado assistant program costs about $3,250 to $5,995 for a short program ([cost breakdown](/blog/much-dental-assistant-training-programs-cost/)), and you can be earning within a few months. For Colorado-specific pay, see [our salary guide](/blog/dental-assistant-salary-colorado/).

## Using assisting as a bridge

Many hygienists started as assistants, and the path makes sense:

- You find out whether you like working in mouths before paying for a degree.
- You earn money while finishing hygiene prerequisites at a community college.
- You get to know hygienists and dentists who can write strong recommendations.
- You arrive at hygiene school already comfortable with instruments, infection control and patients.

If you plan to go this route, keep a record of your hours and duties from the start, and look at hygiene program prerequisites early so you can take them part time while you work.

## Which should you choose?

Choose **assisting** if you want to be working in healthcare within months, prefer hands-on teamwork, or want to try the field before committing to a degree.

Choose **hygiene** if you're ready for a science-heavy, multi-year program, want your own patient schedule, and are aiming for the higher pay ceiling.

Choosing assisting first doesn't close off hygiene later. If you want to start on the assisting side, the [program match quiz](/find-a-program/) shows which Colorado programs fit your schedule and budget.

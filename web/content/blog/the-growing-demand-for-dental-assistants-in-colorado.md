Search "dental assistant" on any job board with your location set to Denver or Colorado Springs, and you'll find a long list of openings. The federal data explains why.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics [projects dental assistant employment to grow 7% between 2025 and 2035](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/dental-assistants.htm), which it calls much faster than average. There were about 388,600 dental assistant jobs in the country in 2025, and BLS expects around 53,000 openings every year.

That 53,000 figure is the one that matters. Most of those openings aren't new positions. They're jobs that open up when someone leaves: an assistant goes back to school for hygiene, moves into office management, relocates or retires. Dental assisting has always been a starting point for many people, so offices are almost always replacing someone. For a newcomer, that steady turnover is good news.

## Why Colorado keeps hiring

The Front Range keeps adding people, and those people need dentists. New practices open in growing suburbs like Castle Rock, Erie and Johnstown. Group practices and dental service organizations have been opening larger multi-chair offices, and each one needs several assistants to run.

Specialists add to it. Orthodontic, pediatric and oral-surgery practices all want assistants with particular skills, and they tend to pay for them. An aging population also keeps schedules full with implants, crowns and periodontal work.

If you want official Colorado numbers rather than national ones, BLS publishes [state and metro wage and employment estimates](https://www.bls.gov/oes/), and the Colorado Department of Labor and Employment has its own labor-market tools.

## Where the openings are

Metro Denver, including Aurora, Lakewood and the northern suburbs, is the biggest market by far and has the widest mix of employers. Our [Denver guide](/locations/denver/) covers the schools there. Colorado Springs is the second big hub, with a lot of military families moving in and out, which keeps turnover high ([Springs guide](/locations/colorado-springs/)). Fort Collins, Loveland, Greeley, Boulder and Longmont add up to a solid third market.

Mountain towns and rural areas post fewer jobs, but they also get fewer applicants. An office in a small town is often more willing to train someone local than to wait for an experienced assistant to move there.

## Demand doesn't mean anyone gets hired

Offices are hiring, but they're still choosy. The applicants who get offers fastest can usually take x-rays from the first day ([Colorado's rule is here](/requirements/)), understand sterilization well enough not to need supervision, and hold a current BLS card. Beyond that, being reliable and calm with nervous patients goes a long way.

Put those together and the outlook for a trained assistant in Colorado is very good.

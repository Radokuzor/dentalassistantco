Most dental assistants work in private practices, but not all. Community health centers, school-based programs, mobile clinics and government facilities also employ assistants, and for many people that work is the most meaningful part of their career.

## Where community dental care happens

**Community health centers.** Nonprofit clinics that serve patients regardless of ability to pay, often with a sliding fee. Many in Colorado include dental clinics alongside medical and behavioral health. Patients often use Health First Colorado (the state's Medicaid program) or are uninsured.

**School-based and outreach programs.** Screenings, fluoride and sealant programs brought to schools and community events. Assistants help set up portable equipment, manage infection control in non-clinical spaces and keep kids calm.

**Mobile dental clinics.** Vans and trailers that reach rural areas, shelters and events. Useful in a state with large rural stretches between cities.

**Government and military facilities.** Military installations in Colorado, such as Fort Carson in Colorado Springs, have dental clinics that hire civilian assistants through official federal channels. These openings appear only now and then and are often open for about a week, so it pays to check regularly.

## What the work looks like

The clinical core is the same as in any office: chairside assisting, x-rays (the same [Colorado radiography rules](/requirements/) apply), infection control and patient care. The differences:

- **Complex cases.** Patients may have gone years without care, so extractions, emergencies and multi-visit treatment plans are more common.
- **More varied patients.** Different languages, cultures, ages and health conditions. [Communication skills](/blog/building-patient-rapport-the-importance-of-communication-in-dental-assisting/) matter even more.
- **More paperwork.** Eligibility, grant reporting and public insurance documentation.
- **Teamwork across disciplines.** In integrated clinics you may coordinate with medical providers or case managers.

## Why assistants choose it

- **Purpose.** You see directly how dental pain affects people's work, school and health, and you help fix it.
- **Learning curve.** The volume and variety of cases make you good quickly.
- **Stable employers.** Larger organizations may offer more structured benefits and schedules than small private offices. Always compare the specific offer.
- **Training.** Community clinics are often among the more willing to hire newer assistants ([getting hired with no experience](/blog/dental-assistant-jobs-no-experience-colorado/)).

## The trade-offs

- Pay can be lower or higher than private practice depending on the organization. Compare offers on the full package ([Colorado pay guide](/blog/dental-assistant-salary-colorado/)).
- Caseloads can be heavy and emotionally demanding.
- Resources are sometimes tight, including older equipment or long waitlists.

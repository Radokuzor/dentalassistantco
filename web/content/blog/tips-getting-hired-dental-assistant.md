Dental offices hire assistants all the time. Turnover is steady, most practices are small, and one missing assistant can shut down a whole chair. So the hard part isn't finding openings. It's being the candidate who looks easy to say yes to.

Here's what that looks like in practice.

## Where Colorado offices find assistants

Most dental practices have fewer than a dozen people and no HR department. The office manager or the dentist handles hiring between patients. That changes where you should look:

- **Your externship or school.** Many assistants get their first job from the office where they did clinical hours, or through a lead from an instructor. If you're still choosing a school, ask each one how graduates find work ([questions to ask](/blog/choose-dental-assisting-school-thats-right/)).
- **Direct outreach.** Dropping off a resume in person, mid-afternoon on a weekday, still works in dentistry. Ask for the office manager, keep it to two minutes, and leave a one-page resume.
- **Job listings.** Our [Colorado job board](/jobs/) lists openings that practices post with us, and you can apply on the page. If nothing fits yet, [join the talent pool](/jobs/#talent-pool) so offices that are hiring can find you.
- **Temp and staffing agencies.** Temping is a good way to see several offices quickly and often turns into a permanent offer.

## The one-page resume

Office managers skim. Put the things that decide the hire near the top:

1. **Qualifications first.** "Qualified to take radiographs in Colorado," your DANB credentials if you have them (RHS, ICE, CDA), and current CPR/BLS. X-ray status is the single most useful line for a new assistant. [Here's what Colorado requires](/requirements/).
2. **Clinical hours.** Where you did your externship or clinic hours, how many hours, and what you did there: four-handed dentistry, sterilization, digital x-rays, impressions.
3. **Software and equipment.** Name the practice software, digital sensors or scanners you've used, even briefly.
4. **Everything else, briefly.** Customer-facing jobs count. Front desk, retail and restaurant work all show you can stay calm with the public, and that matters chairside.

Skip the objective statement. A two-line summary is fine: "New dental assistant, Colorado x-ray qualified, 120 externship hours in a general practice. Available weekdays and Saturdays."

## Interview questions you'll probably hear

You won't get trick questions. Most dental interviews are about whether you're dependable, teachable and good with nervous people. Prepare short, specific answers to these:

**"Why dental assisting?"** Give a real reason, not "I like helping people." Maybe you like hands-on work, or you want healthcare without years of school.

**"Walk me through how you'd turn over a room between patients."** They're checking infection control. Talk through it in order: gloves, disinfecting surfaces, handling instruments for sterilization, barriers, and setting up for the next procedure.

**"Are you qualified to take x-rays in Colorado?"** Answer exactly: which route you completed and whether you have the certificate with you.

**"How would you calm an anxious patient?"** Mention explaining what's happening before you do it, watching for signs of discomfort, and letting the dentist know. We cover this in [building patient rapport](/blog/building-patient-rapport-the-importance-of-communication-in-dental-assisting/).

**"Tell me about a time you made a mistake at work."** Choose a real, small one, say what you did to fix it, and what you changed afterward.

**"What's your availability?"** Be precise. Offices need to know about early mornings, late days and Saturdays.

**"What are your pay expectations?"** Give a range based on current local postings. Our [Colorado salary guide](/blog/dental-assistant-salary-colorado/) explains how to find one.

Ask a few questions of your own too: how new assistants are trained, what a typical day looks like, how many assistants share the schedule, and when reviews happen.

## The working interview

Many offices follow a good conversation with a working interview. You spend a half or full day in the clinic, usually sterilizing, restocking, seating patients and assisting on simple procedures.

A few things to know:

- **Ask whether it's paid.** It's a reasonable question, and it's better to know before you go.
- **Don't try to do everything.** Nobody expects you to know their system. They're watching whether you ask good questions, stay out of the way when you should, and keep a clean field.
- **Stay busy.** When there's a lull, ask what needs restocking or wiping down.
- **Be kind to the front desk.** Staff talk to each other afterward.

## If you're starting with no experience

Nearly every assistant started without experience, so this is normal. General practices with several chairs and community health clinics are usually the most willing to train. Specialty offices (orthodontics, oral surgery) often want a year of general experience first. Our guide to [dental assistant jobs with no experience](/blog/dental-assistant-jobs-no-experience-colorado/) goes deeper.

If you haven't trained yet, a short program is often the fastest way to be hireable, mostly because it gets you the x-ray qualification and supervised chair time. [Compare Colorado programs](/programs/dental-assistant/) or take the [60-second program match](/find-a-program/).

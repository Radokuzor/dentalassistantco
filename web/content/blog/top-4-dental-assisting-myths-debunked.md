Dental assisting is full of assumptions, some from other states, some from TV, and some from people who haven't set foot in a dental office in years. Here are the ones we hear most, checked against Colorado's rules and the data.

## Myth 1: "You need a state license"

**Not in Colorado.** Colorado doesn't license or register dental assistants. The Colorado Dental Board regulates which *tasks* an assistant may do and under what supervision. For some tasks, most notably taking x-rays, you need approved training first. [Full explanation](/blog/dental-assistant-colorado-licensed/).

That's different from many other states, which is where the confusion comes from. If you move, check the new state's rules.

## Myth 2: "You need a college degree"

**No.** Colorado has short programs of about 8 to 13 weeks, and some people train entirely on the job ([the on-the-job route](/blog/become-dental-assistant-colorado-without-going-school/)). Career colleges offer longer certificates with externships and financial aid, but a degree isn't required.

In practice, most employers do prefer someone who's already trained, mostly because trained assistants arrive x-ray qualified and comfortable at the chair.

## Myth 3: "DANB certification is mandatory"

**Not in Colorado, but it's still useful.** Colorado doesn't require the DANB CDA to work as an assistant. DANB's radiography exam (RHS) or CDA is one of the recognized ways to qualify for x-rays, though, and DANB credentials are portable if you move. [Is DANB required in Colorado?](/blog/danb-certification-required-colorado/)

## Myth 4: "Dental assistants just clean teeth"

**That's the hygienist.** Dental hygienists are licensed to clean teeth and assess gum health. Assistants work alongside the dentist during procedures, take x-rays, run infection control and sterilization, take impressions or scans, and look after patients before and after treatment. [Assistant vs. hygienist](/blog/the-difference-between-dental-assistant-and-dental-hygienist-careers/).

## Myth 5: "It's a dead-end job"

**Only if you stay still.** Assistants regularly move into:

- Specialties such as [orthodontics](/blog/orthodontic-assistant-colorado/) and oral surgery
- [Expanded duties](/blog/understanding-the-role-of-expanded-duties-dental-assistants-edda/)
- Lead assistant, office manager or treatment coordinator roles
- Dental hygiene school

Pay follows those moves. [The highest-paying assistant jobs](/blog/highest-paying-dental-assistant-job/).

## Myth 6: "Online programs are just as good"

**For theory, maybe. For hand skills, no.** You can learn terminology and infection control principles online. You can't learn chairside assisting or x-ray technique without supervised hands-on practice. Good hybrid programs combine online coursework with real clinical hours. [What to know about online programs](/blog/can-become-dental-assistant-online/).

## The bottom line

Dental assisting in Colorado is quicker to enter, less regulated and more flexible than many people expect. It still takes real skill, and the assistants who keep learning are the ones whose pay keeps rising.
